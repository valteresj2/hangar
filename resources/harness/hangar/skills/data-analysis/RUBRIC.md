Judge the analysis as a senior analyst who must sign it:

- How was the data collected and in what order? If rows follow time, batches or sources, could an apparent effect (a "best" category, a strong predictor) be the collection period or source in disguise? Was that tested by looking inside each period or group?
- Does the validation imitate how the result will be used? A model meant for future cases should be tested on later data than it was trained on; a random split can flatter it.
- Is every predictor known at the moment of the decision? Anything recorded during or after the outcome leaks it.
- Are the comparisons fair: group sizes shown, the same denominator, no conclusion from a handful of rows, no aggregate that reverses inside the groups?
- Do the recommendations follow from the numbers, with the expected effect and its uncertainty, or do they claim causes the data cannot show?
- Is the headline number honest about its range (interval, sensitivity to a reasonable alternative choice)?
